How much are vehicle registration fees in Vermont?

Following a recent 19.8% legislative fee increase, a standard gas-powered passenger vehicle in Vermont now costs $91.00 for a one-year registration and $167.00 for a two-year registration. However, if you drive an alternative fuel vehicle, you will owe a significant additional Infrastructure Fee to offset your lack of gas tax contributions at the pump.

What affects your Vermont registration fee?

  • Registration Term: You have the option to register your vehicle for either one or two years. Opting for two years offers a marginal discount and saves you a trip to the DMV.
  • Alternative Fuel Infrastructure Fees: Starting in 2025/2026, Vermont enacted new fees for plug-in vehicles. Fully electric vehicles (BEVs) pay an extra $89.00 per year, while Plug-in Hybrids (PHEVs) pay an extra $44.50 per year on top of standard registration costs.
  • Purchase and Use Tax: If you are titling a newly acquired vehicle, Vermont charges a flat 6% tax. Fortunately, any trade-in value is deducted from the purchase price before the tax is calculated, which can save you hundreds of dollars.
  • Titling & Liens: First-time registrations require a $42 Certificate of Title fee. If you finance the vehicle, there is an additional $14 lien recording fee.

How are registration fees calculated in Vermont?

The Vermont DMV does not depreciate your registration fees based on the vehicle's age, weight, or MSRP. It is a strictly flat-rate system based on the powertrain:

  • Gas/Standard Hybrid: $91.00 (1-Year) or $167.00 (2-Year).
  • PHEV (Plug-in Hybrid): Base Reg + Infrastructure Fee ($135.50 for 1-Year or $256.00 for 2-Year).
  • BEV (Fully Electric): Base Reg + Infrastructure Fee ($178.00 for 1-Year or $341.00 for 2-Year).

To calculate a new purchase, simply take your chosen registration fee and add the $42 title fee and 6% Purchase and Use Tax.

What this calculator may not include

  • Safety and Emissions Inspections: Vermont requires an annual state safety and OBD-II emissions inspection. This fee is paid directly to the licensed private inspection station (usually around $45-$60), not to the DMV.
  • Specialty / Vanity Plates: Conservation, agriculture, or personalized vanity plates carry additional annual renewal fees (usually between $26 and $48) on top of your base registration.
  • Town Property Taxes: Vermont does not collect municipal property taxes on vehicles at the DMV level, making the checkout process much simpler than in neighboring states.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the car registration fee in Vermont?

A standard 1-year registration for a gas-powered passenger vehicle is $91.00, while a 2-year registration is $167.00. Electric and plug-in hybrid vehicles pay significantly more due to added infrastructure fees.

Is this calculator accurate for all vehicles in Vermont?

This calculator is highly accurate for standard passenger cars, SUVs, and light non-commercial trucks under 6,100 lbs. It does not calculate specific fees for heavy commercial vehicles, farm trucks, motorhomes, or motorcycles (which cost $58/yr).

Can registration fees in Vermont change every year?

Because Vermont uses a flat-fee schedule, your registration fee does not depreciate as your car ages. The only time the fee changes is if the state legislature passes a bill increasing the base rates, as they recently did with a 19.8% hike to match inflation.

Can I use this calculator for a new car and a used car in Vermont?

Yes. If you are registering a newly acquired vehicle (new from a dealer or used from a private party), select "New Purchase." This will calculate your $42 title fee and your 6% Purchase and Use Tax based on the purchase price.
