How much are vehicle registration fees in Rhode Island?

For standard gas-powered passenger vehicles, a standard two-year registration renewal in Rhode Island costs between $103.50 and $139.50 depending on the vehicle's weight. This total is comprised of a base weight fee, a $40 Department of Transportation (DOT) surcharge, and a $3.50 technology fee. If you drive an electric or hybrid vehicle, you will owe a massive alternative fuel surcharge spanning from $100 to $400 per two-year cycle starting in 2026.

What affects your Rhode Island registration fee?

  • Vehicle Weight: Rhode Island scales its base registration fee based on the vehicle's gross weight. A small car under 4,000 lbs costs $60 for two years, whereas heavier SUVs (up to 6,000 lbs) cost $96.
  • The 2026 DOT Surcharge Increase: Effective January 1, 2026, the state increased the mandatory DOT surcharge applied to all two-year passenger registrations from $30 up to $40.
  • Alternative Fuel Surcharges (NEW for 2026): Because alternative fuel vehicles do not pay standard gas taxes to fund road repairs, Rhode Island implemented heavy new fees in its FY26 budget. BEVs are charged $200 per year ($400 for a 2-year renewal), PHEVs pay $100 per year ($200 at renewal), and standard/mild hybrids pay $50 per year ($100 at renewal).
  • Car Tax Abolishment: The good news for Rhode Island drivers is that the state fully abolished its motor vehicle excise tax effective during the 2022 tax year. You no longer have to pay annual value-based car taxes to your municipality.

How are registration fees calculated in Rhode Island?

The Rhode Island DMV calculates your renewal utilizing this formula:

  • (2-Year Weight Base Fee) + ($40 DOT Surcharge) + ($3.50 Tech Surcharge) + (2-Year EV/Hybrid Surcharge if applicable).

When you purchase a vehicle and register it for the first time, you must also pay a $53.50 Title Fee, a $43.50 Plate Fee for standard two-way plates, and the state's 7.0% Sales Tax on the purchase price (minus any trade-in equity).

What this calculator may not include

  • Safety and Emissions Inspections: Rhode Island requires a biennial safety and emissions inspection, which is generally $55, but this fee is paid directly to the certified mechanic's shop, not the DMV.
  • Vanity and Specialty Plates: Special charity plates, sports team plates, or personalized vanity plates carry additional manufacturing fees (typically around $68 to $120+).
  • Credit Card Surcharges: If you pay online or in-person with a credit card, the state’s third-party processor charges a 2.40% convenience fee (or a minimum of $1.55).

Frequently asked questions

How much is the car registration fee in Rhode Island?

A standard two-year renewal for a gas-powered car usually costs between $103.50 and $139.50 depending on weight. However, alternative fuel vehicles face new surcharges that can push total renewal costs over $500 for a two-year cycle.

Is this calculator accurate for all vehicles in Rhode Island?

This calculator precisely maps standard passenger cars, SUVs, light trucks, and motorcycles. It does not calculate specific fees for heavy commercial vehicles, farm equipment, campers, or trailers which utilize separate prorated and weight-specific charts.

Can registration fees in Rhode Island change every year?

No, standard passenger registrations do not depreciate based on vehicle age in Rhode Island. Your weight-based fee remains locked over the life of the vehicle unless the state legislature officially increases the fee schedule (as they did with the DOT surcharge for 2026).

Can I use this calculator for a new car and a used car in Rhode Island?

Yes. If you are registering a newly purchased car (whether new from a dealer or used from a private party), select "New Purchase." This will calculate your $53.50 title fee, plate issuance fee, and your 7.0% state sales tax based on the net purchase price.
